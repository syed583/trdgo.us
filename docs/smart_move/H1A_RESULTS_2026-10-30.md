## 3. Release-time quality
* The provider gives **only a session label** in `report_time`: `premarket` 223, `postmarket` 217, `unknown` 37 events. **No clock time and no release timestamp exists in this endpoint.** So H1a gives no verified release time.
* `unknown` is concentrated in the future: 34 of the 36 upcoming events are `unknown` (and `source = estimation`, i.e. a projected date, not a company-confirmed one); only **3 of 441 past events** are `unknown`. `source`: company 443, estimation 34 (all upcoming).
* **Label consistency:** the provider's per-event label disagrees with the majority label that the application's own predictions carried for the symbol in **9 events (about 2 %)**: TMUS 4 (provider premarket), SPFI 2, PFS 1, WT 1, KVUE 1. Either the company changed session or one of the two sources is wrong; this is exactly where an independent filing time (H1b, recorded only as "SEC filing acceptance time") is needed. Such cases stay out of the strict set until explained.
* Dates already in the past all have `source = company`; no past event is only an estimate.

## 5. Sample-selection limitations (must accompany every later result)
1. **Survivorship and current-universe bias:** the 50 symbols are companies the application predicted for in Oct 2026; companies delisted, acquired or dropped earlier in the 2-year window are absent. Events are historical but the names are chosen with hindsight of today's universe.
2. **One-season, graded-order history of the universe:** the universe export is built from the predictions of one earnings season; names that only report in other quarters of the cycle (non-standard fiscal calendars) are under-represented.
3. **Timing-only stratification:** no liquidity, sector, size or price control (no volume in the database); thin names may be over- or under-represented. Liquidity will be described afterwards from candle volumes, not used for selection.
4. **Exclusions:** 53 symbols with no usable timing label (6 % of the universe) and 1 tie were removed; if missing timing relates to size, exchange or listing type this biases the sample. ADRs, foreign private issuers and funds are not removed in H1a (no SEC data was read); any such names are identified at H1b (for example by the absence of 8-K filings) and counted then.
5. **Small and clustered:** 50 symbols, about 441 past events, 10 quarters; events cluster on the same few weeks each quarter. Any statistic from it carries wide intervals and is a pilot, not validation.
6. **No result about prediction quality exists yet.** H1a produced event lists only.
