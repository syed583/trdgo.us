## 4. Independent recalculation of peak demand and coverage
Script `scripts/recalc_peak_demand.py` (offline; result file `shadow/peak_demand_recalc.json`). It reads the 441 dated historical events of the 50-name sample as an empirical picture of how a reporting season spreads over days, scales each season to N events, and computes coverage(cap) = sum over days of min(demand, cap) divided by total demand; estimator A is the earlier scaling method, estimator B resamples the 50 symbols with replacement (1,000 draws) to give an interval. Caveat: the sample consists of names that reported in the 13-30 October window, so it describes the timing pattern of exactly those names; real counts are still required.
| Season size N | Peak-day events (A; B 95 % interval) | Coverage at 30 per day | **Coverage at 115 per day** (A; B 95 % interval) | Ceiling for 80 % / 95 % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 590 (a mis-specified "season"; really one rolling queue) | 126 (118-168) | 53 % (43-54 %) | 95 % (88-97 %) | 70 / 115 |
| **878** (events seen in 14 trading days) | 187 (178-248) | 40 % (31-39 %) | **85 % (75-88 %)** | 100 / **170** |
| 1,000 | 213 (204-282) | 37 % (28-35 %) | 81 % (71-83 %) | 115 / 195 |
| 1,200 | 256 (243-341) | 32 % (24-30 %) | 75 % (64-77 %) | 135 / 235 |
Arithmetic checks (exact): tiered plan peak requests per day = 115 x 5 + 30 x 13 + 24 = **989**; approved full-capture plan = 30 x 18 + 24 = **564**. The 990 figure is arithmetically right **for the ceiling it assumes** but the ceiling does not reach 95 % coverage for the 878-name universe.
Requests per day on the busiest days for other ceilings (878 universe): core 115 + full 30 = 989; **core 170 + full 30 = 1,264** (what the sample suggests for about 95 % of 878 events); core 235 + full 30 = 1,589. Against the warmer's 10-18 thousand requests a day this is about +5-10 %, +7-13 % and +9-16 % respectively; none approaches a provider limit (per-minute headroom 1,000,000, concurrency kept at the shared 3, sequential pacing: 1,264 requests take about 42 minutes). Provider cost remains expected zero (flat plan, billing not verified); local storage about 0.3-0.7 GB a year for the extended tier.
**Result of the independent recalculation:**
1. The 990-request peak is correct arithmetic for 115 core + 30 full, but **the claim of 95 % coverage is not supported for the 878-name universe**; the sample says about 85 % (interval 75-88 %), and 95 % needs a core ceiling near 170 per day (about 1,260 requests on peak days).
2. The earlier "53 % coverage with the approved 30 per day" also changes: about 40 % for N = 878.
3. All these numbers rest on 50 names and could move materially; **the real counts from the script decide**. Until then no activation, and no coverage claim.
